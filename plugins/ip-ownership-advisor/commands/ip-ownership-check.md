## Assessment Framework

Analyze the situation above across these dimensions:

### 1. Scope of the Invention Assignment Agreement
- What does the scope language cover? (Personal-time work? Company resources? Related business?)
- Are there explicit carve-outs for pre-existing IP or personal-time inventions?
- Was a prior inventions schedule included, and if so, what was declared?

### 2. Timing and Development Timeline
- When was the invention conceived? (Before or after employment start?)
- When was it reduced to practice? (Before or after employment start?)
- What contemporaneous evidence supports this timeline?

### 3. Relatedness to Employer's Business
- What is the employer's core business?
- Does the invention fall within the same technical domain?
- Could the employer's business "reasonably anticipate" expanding into this area?

### 4. Applicable State Law
- Which state's law governs the agreement?
- Does that state have a statutory employee-invention carve-out (e.g., California Labor Code § 2870)?
- Would the invention qualify for that carve-out?

### 5. Resources Used
- Were company devices, networks, or accounts used in development?
- Was any development done during work hours or at company facilities?

### 6. Risk Summary

After working through the above, provide:
- **Overall risk level:** Low / Medium / High / Very High
- **Key risk factors** identified in this situation
- **Strongest defenses** available based on the facts
- **Evidence gaps** that need to be addressed
- **Recommended immediate next steps** (in priority order)

Keep the assessment concrete and actionable. Where facts are missing, state what information is needed and why it matters.
